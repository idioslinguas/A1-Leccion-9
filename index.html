Vocabulario Clasificado

Food & Drinks: Contables vs Incontables

Los sustantivos contables tienen forma plural (1 banana, 2 bananas). Los sustantivos incontables no se cuentan individualmente (water, rice, money).

¿Cómo contar los sustantivos incontables? (Contenedores)

No podemos decir *two waters* o *three milks*. Usamos **frases de contenedor o medida**:

A bottle of water Una botella de agua
A glass of milk Un vaso de leche
A cup of coffee Una taza de café
A slice of bread/cheese Una rebanada de pan/queso
A bowl of rice/soup Un tazón de arroz/sopa
A kilo of meat/sugar Un kilo de carne/azúcar
A piece of cake/cheese Un trozo de pastel/queso
A loaf of bread Una barra / hogaza de pan
Reglas Gramaticales

Some, Any, Much, Many, How Much & How Many

Aprende la diferencia exacta entre elementos contables e incontables y cuándo utilizar cada cuantificador.

1. Sustantivos Contables (Countable Nouns)

Se pueden contar en números individuales (1, 2, 3...)

Tienen forma singular y plural: a banana $\rightarrow$ three bananas, an egg $\rightarrow$ four eggs.

  • Singular usa a / an (a burger, an egg).
  • Pregunta cantidad con How many? (How many cookies?).
  • Usa many en negativas/preguntas (Not many tomatoes).

2. Sustantivos Incontables (Uncountable Nouns)

Líquidos, sustancias, masa o dinero general

No se pueden contar en unidades ni tienen plural (*waters, *milks, *moneys no existen en inglés). Usan verbos en singular.

  • NO usan a / an directamente (*a water NO).
  • Pregunta cantidad/precio con How much? (How much money?).
  • Usa much en negativas/preguntas (There isn't much sugar).

Cuadro Comparativo: Some, Any, Much, Many

SOME (Un poco de / Algunos)

Se usa en oraciones AFIRMATIVAS con contables en plural e incontables.

Ejemplos:

  • There is some milk in the fridge.
  • There are some bananas on the table.
  • Excepción (Oferta): Would you like some coffee?
ANY (Ningún / Algo de)

Se usa en oraciones NEGATIVAS y PREGUNTAS.

Ejemplos:

  • There isn't any cheese.
  • Are there any eggs left?
  • I don't have any money today.
MUCH / HOW MUCH

Para sustantivos INCONTABLES (líquidos, arroz, dinero, etc.).

Ejemplos:

  • How much water do you drink?
  • There isn't much sugar left.
  • How much is this sandwich?
MANY / HOW MANY

Para sustantivos CONTABLES EN PLURAL (bananas, eggs, burgers).

Ejemplos:

  • How many apples do we need?
  • There aren't many cookies in the jar.
  • How many potatoes are there?

A Special Birthday Dinner at the Bistro

Paragraph 1 • Planning the Party

Today is Sarah’s 25th birthday, and her friends are preparing a surprise dinner at her favorite Italian bistro. Her brother, Mark, checks the kitchen pantry to make a shopping list. "How much rice do we have?" asks Mark. His sister replies, "There is some rice in the cup, but there isn’t any sugar for the dessert. We also need three red tomatoes, two fresh potatoes, and some cheese."

Paragraph 2 • Grocery Shopping

Mark goes to the local supermarket with his friend Leo. Mark asks, "How many bananas should we buy?" Leo says, "Let's buy six bananas, five burgers, and a bag of chocolate cookies for the guests." At the checkout, Mark checks his wallet. He doesn't have much money in cash, but he has enough credit on his card to buy a bottle of fresh milk and two cartons of orange juice.

Paragraph 3 • Cooking Together

Back home, they start cooking the main dishes. Leo prepares a giant bowl of pasta with meat sauce and chops one crisp tomato for the salad. Mark boils four eggs for the appetizer and pours some water into a big pot. "Are there any sandwiches left in the fridge?" asks Leo. Mark answers, "No, there aren't any sandwiches, but there is some delicious pizza on the table!"

Paragraph 4 • The Celebration

At 7:00 PM, Sarah arrives and is completely surprised! Everyone shouts "Happy Birthday!" They sit around the dining table, enjoying hot coffee, sweet fruit, and fresh bread. Sarah smiles and says, "There is so much joy and love here tonight. Thank you all!" It was a memorable party filled with great food and good friends.
